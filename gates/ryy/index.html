$R_{yy}$

rotations· 2 qubits ·1 parameter

$yy$ Rotation

Two-qubit rotation generated by $Y \otimes Y$.

Open in Quirk ↗ with $\theta = 2\pi t$

The $R_{yy}$ gate rotates in the $YY$ interaction basis by angle $\theta$.

$$ R_{yy}(\theta) = \exp\left(-i\frac{\theta}{2}Y\otimes Y\right) $$

In the computational basis:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} \cos\tfrac{\theta}{2} & 0 & 0 & i\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2} \\ 0 & \cos\tfrac{\theta}{2} & -i\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & -i\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2} & \cos\tfrac{\theta}{2} & 0 \\ i\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2} & 0 & 0 & \cos\tfrac{\theta}{2} \end{bmatrix} $$

Properties

  • Entangling for $\theta$ that are not an integer multiple of $\pi$.
  • Related to $R_{xx}$ by single-qubit phase rotations: $R_{yy}(\theta) = (S \otimes S)\,R_{xx}(\theta)\,(S^\dagger \otimes S^\dagger)$.
  • Generated by a $YY$ interaction Hamiltonian.

Usage

  • Entangling layers in variational algorithms.
  • Basis-changed implementations of $XX$ or $ZZ$ couplings.

$(a, b, c)$ means the gate equals $e^{i(a\,XX + b\,YY + c\,ZZ)}$ up to single-qubit gates, reduced to the chamber $\pi/4 \ge a \ge b \ge |c|$ (the convention of the canonical gate, Qiskit and Cirq). The $a = \pi/4$ face is a mirror: $(\pi/4, b, c)$ and $(\pi/4, b, -c)$ are the same gate. Drag the figure to rotate it, double-click to reset.
